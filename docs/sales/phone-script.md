# Phone script: calling practices

For calling UK practices that don't know us yet. Aim: a 15-minute
video call or visit with the person who owns the problem. Don't try to
close the sale on the first call.

## Before you call

- **Check the number isn't on the Corporate TPS**, the UK do-not-call
  list for businesses, at tpsservices.co.uk. Don't call a number that's
  registered.
- **Look the practice up first:** website, the treatments they push
  (implants, Invisalign, cosmetic), and their practice software if the
  site shows it (e.g. a Dentally booking link).
- **Best times:** 12:30–1:30 (lunch) or after 4:30. Avoid Monday
  mornings.
- **Log every call** in your spreadsheet: practice, who you spoke to,
  outcome, next step and date.

## 1. Reception (the gatekeeper)

> "Hi, my name's [name], I'm a dentist. Could I speak to whoever looks
> after treatment plans and follow-up, normally the practice manager or
> treatment coordinator?"

If asked what it's about:

> "It's about patients who've been given a treatment quote but haven't
> booked yet. I've built something with other UK dentists that follows
> them up, and I'd love two minutes with whoever handles that."

If they're busy or out:

> "No problem. What's their name, and when's a good time to catch them?"

Write the name down and call back then.

## 2. The decision-maker (practice manager, TCO or owner)

**Opener, under 20 seconds:**

> "Thanks for taking the call. I'm [name], a dentist. I'll be quick.
> Like most practices, we found patients who say 'I'll think about it'
> after a treatment quote often never come back, and nobody has time to
> keep chasing them. We built TreatmentTrack to do that follow-up for us.
> Can I ask you two quick questions to see if it's relevant to you?"

**Questions.** Listen more than you talk:

1. "Roughly how many private treatment quotes do you give a month:
   implants, aligners, crowns, that kind of thing?"
2. "When a patient says they'll think about it, what happens next? Who
   follows them up, and how many times?"
3. "Do you know roughly how much quoted treatment is sitting unbooked
   right now?" (Most don't, and that's the hook.)
4. "Which practice software are you on: Dentally, SOE, R4 or CareStack?"

**Connect it to what they said:**

> "That's exactly why we built it. You give it the quotes, typed in or
> imported straight from [their software], and it sends polite texts or
> emails in the practice's name at the times you choose: a few days
> later, a week, a month. Every message has an opt-out, and it stops the
> moment the patient books or says no. Once they book, it tracks the case
> through to completion, so nobody slips through. And you can see exactly
> how much it's brought back."

**The ask:**

> "We're taking on a small number of founding practices at the moment.
> Could I show you on a quick 15-minute video call, or pop in? I can show
> it with an example of your own quotes. What suits you better, [day] or
> [day]?"

Offer two specific times. That gets a yes more often than "when are you
free?".

## 3. Common objections

**"Send me an email."**
> "Of course. What's the best address? I'll send a one-page summary. So
> it doesn't get lost, can I pencil in 15 minutes on [day] to talk it
> through? If it's not for you, just tell me then."

**"We already have recall / reminders in Dentally (or SOE)."**
> "Those are great for check-ups and appointment reminders. This is
> different: it chases one specific quote, like the £2,800 implant
> someone said they'd think about, until they book or say no, and it
> stops automatically when they do. Practice software isn't really built
> to do that."

**"Our treatment coordinator already follows up."**
> "That's great, you're ahead of most practices. How many times do they
> manage to chase each patient when it's busy? This does the routine
> chasing so your TCO can spend their time on the patients who reply and
> want to talk."

**"Is it GDPR-compliant? We can't just text patients."**
> "It's built around that. Each quote records that the patient was
> offered an opt-out, which is the PECR soft opt-in for following up a
> quote. Every message has a STOP option that's honoured straight away,
> patient records are stored in the UK, and there's a data processing
> agreement for the practice."

**"How much is it?"**
> "From £99 a month for a single dentist, and £199 for a whole practice.
> Founding practices get a better rate. To put it in context, one
> recovered implant or Invisalign case covers it for well over a year."

**"We're too busy right now."**
> "Completely understand, and that's exactly when quotes go unchased.
> When would be a better time for me to call back: next week, or next
> month?" (Log the date and call back then.)

**"Not interested."**
> "No problem at all, thanks for your time. Can I ask, is that because
> follow-up isn't an issue for you, or just the timing?" (If they say
> don't call again, mark them as do-not-contact in the spreadsheet and
> don't contact them again.)

## 4. Voicemail (under 25 seconds)

> "Hi [name], it's [your name]. I'm a dentist calling about patients who
> get a treatment quote but don't book. I've built something with other
> UK dentists that follows them up for you. I'll send a short email too.
> My number's [number]. Thanks!"

## 5. After the call

- **Same day**, send the one-page leaflet with a short email that
  mentions something they told you ("You mentioned around 20 implant
  consultations a month…").
- **If they agreed a call:** send a calendar invite straight away.
- **No reply after a week:** one follow-up call or email. After two
  tries with no answer, move on and try again in a couple of months.

## Ground rules

- **Be honest about where we are:** "launching with founding
  practices". Never claim customers, results or features we don't have.
- **Don't take payment** until billing and text messaging are switched
  on for real practices (see docs/GO-LIVE.md).
- **Never ask a practice to send patient lists** by email or WhatsApp.
  Real patient data goes into TreatmentTrack only once the practice has
  signed up and accepted the data processing agreement.
